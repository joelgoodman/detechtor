## What this audit does NOT tell you

Read this section before quoting any number above.

**It cannot detect a false-positive pattern.** The audit asks whether a technology sits in
the right category. It is blind to whether the technology is being detected correctly. Ask
any reviewer "is Ghost correctly categorised as CMS?" and the answer is yes — Ghost genuinely
is a CMS. Meanwhile its pattern is `html:["ghost"]`, which matches every `btn-ghost` CSS class
on every page, and it "fires" on 15.8% of universities. **The category is right and the
detection is garbage, and nothing in this audit can see that.** Those go to UNI-237.

**24 suspected over-broad patterns** and
**43 names that over-claim** were collected as a side effect and
recorded in `docs/uni-235-deferred-findings.json`. They are NOT fixed here.

**Prevalence is homepage-only.** A technology firing on zero institutions has not been proven
absent — it may live on interior pages (UNI-225) or be present and undetected. Zero means
deprioritised, never absent.

**Most decisions were machine-made.** Categories were adjudicated by batched model review
with human adjudication on the consequential and contested cases. That is a real review, but
it is not the same as a human having read all 6,347 rows, and it should not be
described as if it were.

**A reviewer's "OK" is weaker evidence than its "FLAG".** Measured, not assumed: a pass that
flagged wrongly-filed entries accurately then waved through image CDNs and lazy-loading
utilities as "JavaScript frameworks". Re-checking only the approvals overturned 29 of 74.
Any future audit of this shape must re-check what passed, not only what failed.

**Known-wrong and not fixed.** `Reveal.js` (a presentation library) and `Stitches` (CSS-in-JS)
survived a strict re-audit and are still filed as JavaScript frameworks. Left because they fire
on 5 and 0 institutions respectively — diminishing returns, not correctness.

**`Unclassified` is 417 technologies we examined and could not identify.** That is
deliberate and it is an improvement: previously they made confident false claims ("this is a
JavaScript framework"). Now they assert nothing. It is an invitation to identify them, not a
finished state.

**Being irrelevant is not being wrong.** `Reviews` and `Shipping & Logistics` were reviewed and
changed nothing: they hold genuine review platforms and genuine parcel carriers. No university
runs them, but they are correctly categorised. Whether we should carry those patterns at all is
a different question, and it belongs to UNI-237.
